Who I Am & What I Do
My name is Dr. Qashtalani Haramaini, S.T., M.T.

I'm an energy systems professional with 10+ years in Indonesia's electricity sector, specializing in power distribution, network reliability, and efficiency improvement — including technical and non-technical loss reduction.

My experience spans both a state-owned utility (PT PLN) and the private sector (ENGIE), combining operational leadership with applied work in energy efficiency, AI/data analytics, and the green-energy transition. I coordinate multi-stakeholder programmes across government bodies, industry, universities, and local authorities — and work fluently in Bahasa Indonesia and English.

I hold a Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering (Power Systems) from Universitas Indonesia and am pursuing a second Ph.D. at Monash University under the RACE for 2030 programme (urban design & the energy transition). I also founded EnVisor, an AI-powered energy-efficiency advisor, and Electra Academy (electraacademy.com), a practitioner-focused electrical & energy skills academy.

What excites me — the energy transition. I'm an enthusiast of smart grids, distributed solar & net metering, demand-side management, dynamic tariffs, AI for utilities, and turning idle energy into value. I'm actively looking for challenges in advising the energy transition, and love connecting with researchers, engineers, and founders building Indonesia's clean-energy future — if that's you, let's talk.

Open Thoughts — Energy
Energy Transition
Indonesia's grid isn't ready for rooftop solar — and net metering is making it worse

The current 1:0.65 export ratio penalizes industrial prosumers while grid infrastructure remains underprepared for bidirectional flow. My 21 MW Bekasi case showed a cumulative consumer loss of −$2,102 over two years. Indonesia needs dynamic net metering — not a static punitive multiplier.

Based on IEEE CGEE 2022 · Bekasi 2020–2022
Renewable Energy
PLN's idle geothermal capacity is a missed opportunity at national scale

Indonesia has 40% of the world's geothermal potential but uses less than 10%. Paired with the right monetization model, its baseload stability could transform PLN's finances. EnerBit explores one path: regulated Bitcoin mining as intermediate value capture while renewable buildout continues.

Personal thesis · EnerBit research 2025
Smart Grid
Time-of-Use tariffs could cut Java-Madura-Bali peak costs by Rp 3.6 trillion a year

30 industrial customers in PLN Bekasi showed mid-peak demand elasticity of −0.3 — they will shift load when the incentive is real. That's a 2.59% daily generation-cost reduction across the interconnection. Dynamic pricing needs political will more than technology.

Dynamic tariff scenario analysis · UI 2018
Open Thoughts — Economy
Digital Economy
Bitcoin is not a currency — it's a settlement layer for sovereign-scale value storage

Countries with unstable fiat aren't using Bitcoin as a medium of exchange; they use it as a hedge against monetary-policy failure. Indonesia's position is stronger, but the principle holds: states that hold Bitcoin early may gain structural advantages. El Salvador and Bhutan are running the experiment.

Personal view · open for debate
Islamic Economics
The riba prohibition is ultimately about risk-sharing — and Bitcoin respects it

Classical scholars banned guaranteed returns on capital because they distort risk distribution. Bitcoin, a fixed-supply asset with no guaranteed yield, aligns more naturally with Islamic economic principles than fractional-reserve banking. The debate is about what unit best represents productive participation without exploitation.

Personal view · discourse welcome
Indonesia Economy
Energy subsidies are a transfer from future taxpayers to present consumers

Electricity subsidies exceed Rp 70 trillion annually, disproportionately benefiting wealthier, higher-consumption households. A well-designed TOU + targeted-subsidy system would be more equitable and fiscally sustainable. The politics are hard — but so is the cost of inaction as the global transition accelerates.

Energy policy · data from BPS & ESDM
Open Thoughts — AI
AI in Utilities
PLN doesn't need better dashboards — it needs AI agents that can act on what they see

Every digitalization wave builds better visualizations but never closes the loop to action. An operator sees a loss anomaly — then what? The next frontier is agentic AI that detects the anomaly, cross-references history, generates a work order, and notifies the field team, with no human middle step.

From multi-agent tooling · PLN context
AI Adoption
The biggest barrier to AI in Indonesian SOEs isn't technology — it's accountability

When an AI system makes a wrong recommendation that causes operational loss, who is responsible — the engineer, the vendor, or the system? Until there are clear accountability frameworks for government-linked companies, adoption stays slow regardless of capability. It's a governance problem before it's a technology problem.

Personal observation · PLN transformation
Machine Learning
ML can catch electricity thieves better than experienced inspectors — here's why

Moving a fraud-detection hit rate from 15% to 77% wasn't about a brilliant model — it was because inspectors work from intuition and social pressure, while the model works from patterns. ML doesn't get tired, know the customer, or fear conflict. AI wins most where human judgment is biased by relationships.

Frequently Asked

Straight answers about what I do

The questions people ask most often when they are looking for an energy consultant or expert advisor on the energy transition, electric vehicles, or AI in the power sector.

Who is Dr. Qashtalani Haramaini?

Dr. Qashtalani Haramaini, S.T., M.T. is an Indonesian energy systems expert and researcher. He holds a Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering (Power Systems) from Universitas Indonesia and is completing a second Ph.D. at Monash University under the RACE for 2030 Cooperative Research Centre, researching Indonesia's electric-vehicle ecosystem. He is Energy Transaction Manager at PT PLN (Persero) with more than ten years in Indonesia's electricity sector, and the founder of EnVisor and Electra Academy.

What makes him an expert in Indonesia's energy transition?

He combines a doctorate in power systems with operational responsibility inside PT PLN (Persero), Indonesia's state electricity utility — so his research is validated on live distribution networks rather than in simulation only. His peer-reviewed work covers distributed solar PV siting and economics, net-metering policy for medium-voltage industrial consumers, Time-of-Use and dynamic tariffs, demand-side management, and load forecasting for tropical grids — the core policy levers of the energy transition. He has 10 peer-reviewed papers, 61 citations and an h-index of 4.

What is his research on electric vehicles (EV)?

His current doctoral research at Monash University — Pathways to Advancing Sustainable Transportation: A Focus on Indonesia's EV Ecosystem — is carried out under the RACE for EVs program and supervised at Monash Art, Design and Architecture. It examines what Indonesia's charging infrastructure, electricity tariffs and urban form must become for electric vehicles to scale nationally.

What has he done with AI and data analytics in energy?

He built a machine-learning electricity fraud-detection system at PT PLN that raised the field-inspection hit rate from roughly 15% to roughly 77%, recovering lost revenue on a non-smart-grid network — work that won 3rd place at the PLN National Innovation Awards 2024 and made him PLN West Java Innovation Champion 2025. Other applied AI work includes CERDAS, a complaint-prediction model; medium-term load forecasting with neural networks; a tamper-aware smart meter; and satellite night-lights plus GIS analysis of electrical infrastructure under the Esri Fellowship 2026. He also founded EnVisor, an AI-powered energy-efficiency advisor.

What does he consult and advise on?

He is engaged as an energy consultant and expert advisor across six areas: energy transition strategy and policy; EV ecosystems and charging infrastructure; AI and data analytics for utilities; distribution performance and loss reduction; solar PV siting, economics and technical review; and executive training and capacity building. Clients include utilities and system operators, regulators and government bodies, renewable developers, investors, industrial energy users, and universities. See the full advisory page.

Does he take on international clients outside Indonesia?

Yes. He works in English and Bahasa Indonesia, remotely or on site, and has an established international footprint: doctoral research at Monash University in Australia under the RACE for 2030 Cooperative Research Centre, an Australia Awards short course at Griffith University, IEEE professional membership, and conference presentations in Kuala Lumpur, Bali, Istanbul and Nagoya. He is based in Indramayu and Jakarta, Indonesia (UTC+7) — a time zone that overlaps the Asia-Pacific working day and the European morning.

Is he available for consulting or advisory work on the energy transition?

Yes. He is open to research partnerships, AI collaborations and advisory or consulting engagements on Indonesia's energy transition, EV ecosystem and AI for utilities. He works in Bahasa Indonesia and English and is based in Indramayu and Jakarta, Indonesia. Reach him on LinkedIn or by email.
